In Their Own Words

How They Got Here

Fifteen years of service each, on average, and not one of them took the straight route.

Executive Chef & Founder

Julian Rivera

Julian cooked his way up through fourteen dining rooms before opening this one, and the thing he kept being told he lacked was scale. He had plenty of it — twenty-two covers a night, a full team, a network of suppliers on speed dial. What he did not have was the time to cook the four dishes he actually thought about.

Stackly is built around a deliberately short menu for the same reason. Fewer dishes, bought better, cooked by someone who is still in the kitchen at two in the morning when the sauce is not right.

“Restraint is not the absence of ambition. It is ambition with somewhere to put it.”
Trained
Lyon, France — classically trained, three years
Before
Mexico City, Madrid, and a decade in London
Style
Modern French with Latin warmth; live-fire cooking
Writes
The menu, each morning, after the market

Sous Chef & Pastry

Amélie Chen

Amélie spent four years in a Lyon pâtisserie making the kind of pastry that looks ornamental and eats like nothing at all. She left because she kept being asked to make things prettier, and she wanted to make things tastier.

She runs the entire dessert programme here — not just the final course. The bread, the pre-dessert cheese course, the sorbets and the petit fours on the counter all come out of her section, which means the sweet end of the menu is cooked by the person who also wrote it.

“Sugar is not a flavour. It is a lever — I use it to make the fruit taste more like itself.”
Trained
Lyon, France — pâtisserie diploma
Owns
Dessert, bread, petits fours and the counter selection
Style
Modern pâtisserie, low sugar, heavy on acidity
Known for
A gold leaf crumble that has not yet been explained

Chef de Partie

Malik Osei

Malik learned fire before he learned knife skills, cooking over charcoal in Accra where the smoke was not a technique so much as the only heat available. Eight years in European kitchens taught him to hold that heat to a degree and a half — but he cooks by smell before he cooks by thermometer, and has never once needed the second method.

He owns the hearth, which means he also owns the smoke that reaches the dining room. He is the reason there is a faint haze in the room at eight o'clock and the reason the meat tastes faintly of cherry wood.

“You cannot buy smoke. You can only learn when the wood is ready and then not look away.”
Grew up
Accra, Ghana — charcoal cookery from childhood
Refined in
Basel and Copenhagen, eight years across kitchens
Owns
The hearth, the butchery and every fire-cooked plate
Style
Whole-beast cookery, West African spice, hardwood smoke

Signature Dishes

The Dish Each Chef Signs

Every chef here owns one plate outright. None of them will hand it to a stations cook.

Julian Rivera

Smoked Onion Jus

Onions roasted until they collapse, then deglazed with a fortified wine and reduced for close to three hours. Finished tableside with a spoon of cultured cream.

On the menu: course five, every evening

Amélie Chen

Gold Leaf Crumble

Caramelised milk crumb under a spun-sugar shell. One leaf of gold, added at the pass, mostly because it looks expensive.

On the menu: the dessert course, seven nights a week

Malik Osei

Cherry Wood Smoke

Forty-eight hours over applewood, six hours over cherry, carved to order. The smoke is the seasoning; everything else is restraint.

On the menu: chef's special menu only

The Craft

Four Principles We Cook By

The techniques change with the dish. These do not.

Source It Closer

Nine farms and one day-boat fisherman inside a hundred miles. Shorter journeys mean produce that tastes like itself.

Respect The Time

Stock, bread and reductions are started the day before. Nothing on a plate is faster than it needs to be.

Cook With Fire

A wood hearth, not gas, for anything that benefits from smoke. Char is a seasoning, not an accident.

Edit Ruthlessly

Most of what we plate is taken away. Three good elements beat nine busy ones.

Training & Apprenticeships

How To Get A Job Here

We take on two stages a year. No CV, no degree, no portfolio required — just the willingness to be wrong in front of us for six weeks.

1

Stage With Us

Two unpaid-to-expenses stages a year, each two to six weeks long. You work the real menu at real hours, including the part where you are on the knife station and everything hurts.

2

Learn Every Section

Nobody here owns a station in year one. You rotate larder, sauces, grill and pastry until you can read a full service blind. It is slower than it should be and it is the whole point.

3

Take A Section

You get a station, an older cook to answer to, and the real consequence for what comes out of it. Chef de partie by the second year, if you have earned it.

4

Get Paid Properly

We pay above the city rate at every level, review twice a year, and pay for the exams. Four of our current twenty-two came up through this process.

Stages run in March and September

Write to us with one paragraph about the last thing you cooked that you are proud of, and what you would do differently now. That is the entire application.
